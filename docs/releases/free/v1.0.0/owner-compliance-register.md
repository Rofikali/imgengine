## Proposed decision rule and trade-offs

1. Owner separately approves or rejects the governance proposal in writing. Until formally adopted, independent review is required. Acceptance of this document into source control records the proposal only.
2. Limit any self-assessment route to the documented static Free scope. Identify evidence, law in force, responsible owner, residual risks and next review for each row. Attach dated account evidence and rehearsal outcomes privately. Resolve mandatory obligations and factual gaps before signing.
3. Escalate unresolved material questions to a qualified Indian advocate. In particular, clarify SPDI/sole-proprietor scope, CERT-In log retention and reporting, cross-border provider arrangements, or intermediary/consumer status where the facts are disputed. An owner's risk acceptance cannot waive an applicable duty.
4. Record exact notice revision and reviewed provider configuration. Owner signs as operator performing self-assessment. Only an actual independent review can be recorded as such. A material change invalidates the affected assessment.
5. A separate reviewed governance implementation would be needed to revise any policy or guard. Preserve required CI, privacy checks, license review, artifact provenance, independent technical checks, branch protection, post-freeze owner decision, host checks and rollback. Keep release blocked if evidence is missing. Set the real effective date, rebuild and verify the final digest, then obtain separate explicit deployment authorization.

Self-assessment can reduce routine cost and latency for a narrow free static tool. It places interpretation, self-review bias, continuing monitoring and recordkeeping on the sole operator. Independent review provides challenge and specialist judgment, with additional expense and lead time. Targeted advice on the material questions above is the proposed cost control. An unresolved high-impact legal issue keeps the gate blocked under either route.
